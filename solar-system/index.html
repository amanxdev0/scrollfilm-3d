Stop 1 · The galaxy

The Milky Way

100,000 light-years across. Somewhere between 100 and 400 billion stars — and our entire solar system is one tiny glowing dot in one of its spiral arms. That pulsing marker is us.

Stop 2 · Inbound

One star among billions.

We leave the galactic view and fall toward a single, ordinary yellow dwarf — neighbours like Sirius and Betelgeuse shining in the dark around it. Ordinary, except it's ours.

Stop 3 · The star

Sol — the Sun

99.86% of all mass in the solar system. 5,500°C at the surface, 15 million°C at the core — 1.3 million Earths could fit inside it. Everything from here on orbits this furnace.

Stop 4 · Planet I

Mercury

The swift planet — a year here lasts just 88 days. No atmosphere to hold heat, so it swings from −180°C at night to 430°C by day. No moons.

Stop 5 · Planet II

Venus

The hottest planet — 465°C, hotter than Mercury, wrapped in crushing CO₂ skies. It spins backwards, and one Venusian day outlasts its entire year. No moons.

Stop 6 · Planet III · Home

Earth

The only known world with life — 71% ocean, one magnetic shield, one perfect distance from the Sun.
